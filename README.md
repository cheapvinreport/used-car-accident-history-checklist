# Used Car Accident History Checklist

## How to Identify Unreported Accident Damage Before Buying a Used Car

A clean vehicle history report does not necessarily mean a vehicle has never been involved in an accident.

Some collisions and repairs never appear in CARFAX, AutoCheck, or other vehicle history databases because reporting sources may not have documented or shared the information.

This checklist helps used-car buyers identify potential warning signs before making a purchase.

## 1. Review the Vehicle History

- [ ] Verify the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN).
- [ ] Check for reported accidents and damage.
- [ ] Review title brands, including salvage and rebuilt titles.
- [ ] Look for mileage inconsistencies.
- [ ] Check available ownership and service records.

## 2. Inspect the Exterior

- [ ] Compare paint color and texture across body panels.
- [ ] Look for uneven gaps around doors, hood, and trunk.
- [ ] Inspect for overspray on rubber seals and plastic trim.
- [ ] Check for signs of replaced body panels.
- [ ] Look for unusual welding or repair marks.

## 3. Check Structural and Safety Components

- [ ] Inspect accessible structural areas for previous repairs.
- [ ] Check for evidence of airbag deployment or replacement.
- [ ] Examine seat belts and safety system warning lights.
- [ ] Ask about previous collision repairs.
- [ ] Have a qualified professional inspect suspected structural damage.

## 4. Verify Repair Documentation

- [ ] Request maintenance and repair invoices.
- [ ] Ask whether previous damage was repaired privately.
- [ ] Compare the seller's statements with available records.
- [ ] Investigate unexplained gaps in the vehicle's history.

## 5. Arrange an Independent Inspection

A professional pre-purchase inspection can reveal issues that may not appear in a vehicle history report.

Consider having the vehicle inspected by an independent mechanic or collision repair specialist before completing the purchase.

## Why Some Accidents Never Appear in Reports

Accident records may be missing because:

- Repairs were paid for privately without an insurance claim.
- The repair facility did not provide information to reporting services.
- The incident was not documented by police or another reporting source.
- Records were delayed or unavailable when the report was generated.

A clean report should be interpreted as **no relevant accidents found in the available records**, rather than proof that the vehicle has never been damaged.
